# What is the best flooring for a basement in Ontario?

For most Ontario basements the best flooring is rigid-core luxury vinyl plank (SPC or WPC): it is waterproof-rated, sits happily on concrete, and shrugs off the humidity swings a below-grade room goes through every year. Laminate can work in a dry basement with the right underlay, while solid hardwood and most carpet should stay upstairs.

## Why is a basement different from the rest of the house?

A basement floor is a concrete slab that sits on the ground, and the ground in Southern Ontario is never truly dry. Even a basement that has never flooded breathes moisture up through the slab, especially in spring and after a heavy summer storm. Add a furnace, a laundry room and the odd window well, and you have a room that is cooler, damper and less stable than any floor upstairs.

That changes what "good flooring" means. Upstairs you choose on looks, feel and budget. In a basement, the first question is always: **what happens to this floor when it gets damp?** A material that swells, cups or grows mould the first wet April is the wrong material no matter how good the price was.

Two things follow from that. First, the floor itself has to tolerate moisture. Second, whatever goes down has to be installed the way a basement needs it, which usually means a proper look at the slab before anyone opens a box of planks.

## Is luxury vinyl plank the best basement flooring?

For most homes we see across Mississauga and the GTA, yes. Rigid-core luxury vinyl plank, sold as SPC (stone-plastic composite) or WPC (wood-plastic composite), is the floor we recommend for basements more than any other, and for a few plain reasons:

- **It is waterproof-rated.** The plank itself does not absorb water, so a spill, a leaky washer hose or a damp slab does not swell it the way it would a wood-based product.
- **It handles concrete well.** Click-lock vinyl floats over the slab, so there is no glue curing against cold concrete and no nailing into it.
- **It stays flat through the seasons.** A rigid core moves far less with humidity than laminate or wood, which matters in a room that is humid in July and bone-dry in January.
- **It is warm enough underfoot.** Vinyl is not as cold as tile, and an underlay with a built-in vapour barrier takes the chill off further.
- **It looks like wood.** Modern plank formats, bevels and textures mean a finished basement no longer has to look like a utility room.

SPC is the stiffer, denser of the two and tends to hide small slab imperfections better and dent less. WPC is a little softer and quieter underfoot. Either is a sound basement choice; the one that fits depends on how the room will be used.

## Can you put laminate in a basement?

You can, with conditions. Laminate is a wood-fibre board with a photographic wear layer on top. The top is tough, but the core is still wood, and wood swells when it stays wet. Some newer laminates are sold as water-resistant and will survive a spill that is wiped up the same day; they will not survive a slab that stays damp or a basement that takes on water.

Laminate makes sense in a basement when:

1. The slab is dry and tested, not just "it has never flooded".
2. A dedicated underlay with a vapour barrier goes down first.
3. The room is finished living space, not a laundry or utility area.
4. You want the harder, more scratch-resistant surface laminate offers over vinyl, say for a busy playroom.

If any of those is a maybe, rigid-core vinyl is the safer call for about the same look.

## What about engineered hardwood, carpet and tile below grade?

**Engineered hardwood** is the one real-wood product that can go below grade, because its plywood core moves less than solid boards. It still needs a dry, tested slab, a moisture barrier and careful acclimation, and a wet basement will damage it. We treat it as a "finished, dry basement only" option for homeowners who want real wood underfoot.

**Solid hardwood** should stay upstairs. Solid boards expand and contract with humidity far more than a basement allows, and most manufacturers will not warranty them below grade.

**Carpet** is warm and quiet, which is why so many older GTA basements have it, but it holds moisture and odour and is the first thing to go after a leak. If you want softness, a rug over vinyl gives you the feel without the risk.

**Tile** is fully waterproof and a fine choice for a basement bathroom or laundry. In a living area it is cold and hard, and it still needs a flat, sound slab underneath.

## What do GTA basements need before any floor goes down?

The floor is only as good as the slab under it. Before we recommend a material for a basement in Mississauga, Brampton, Oakville or anywhere else in the GTA, we want to know three things:

- **Is the slab dry?** Moisture can be tested rather than guessed. A reading tells us whether vinyl is fine, whether laminate is an option, or whether the basement needs a drainage or waterproofing fix before any floor is worth installing.
- **Is the slab flat?** Click-lock floors need a flat surface. High spots and dips cause planks to rock and joints to open, so levelling compound is sometimes part of the job.
- **Where is the water going to come from?** Window wells, the laundry area, a sump pit and the furnace all tell us where to be careful and where a waterproof product is non-negotiable.

## How do you choose between the options for your basement?

Ask yourself, in order:

1. **Has the basement ever been wet, or might it be?** If yes, or you are not sure, choose rigid-core vinyl (or tile in wet rooms) and stop here.
2. **Is it finished living space with a dry, tested slab?** Then laminate or engineered hardwood join the list.
3. **What will the room be used for?** A playroom or gym favours a harder, denser surface; a family room or bedroom can take a softer WPC.
4. **What do you want it to look like?** All of these come in wood-look planks today, so looks rarely decide it on their own.

For the majority of Ontario basements that leaves rigid-core luxury vinyl plank as the answer, with laminate as a reasonable choice in the dry, finished ones.

## FAQ

### Is vinyl plank flooring really waterproof in a basement?

Rigid-core vinyl plank is waterproof-rated: the planks themselves do not absorb water or swell. That is different from the room being waterproof. Standing water can still get under a floating floor and sit on the slab, so a vinyl floor protects against spills and damp, not against a flood that is never cleaned up.

### Do I need underlay under vinyl in a basement?

Usually yes. Many rigid-core planks come with a pad attached, but a separate underlay with a vapour barrier adds a moisture layer between the slab and the floor, softens the feel and quiets footsteps. Follow the plank manufacturer's guidance on which underlay is allowed, because doubling up pads can void a warranty.

### Can I install basement flooring directly over concrete?

Floating click-lock vinyl and laminate can go over a concrete slab that is dry, clean and flat. Glue-down products and any wood-based product need a moisture check first, and most need a vapour barrier. A slab with dips or high spots should be levelled before any plank goes down.

### How long does basement flooring last?

A rigid-core vinyl floor installed over a prepared slab routinely lasts well over a decade in a basement. Laminate lasts about as long in a dry basement and far less in a damp one. The slab preparation and the moisture situation decide the lifespan more than the product does.
